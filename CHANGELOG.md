## 1.6.0 -- unreleased

Wave 2 of the roadmap, per the contributing docs' enhancement-proposal page: the protocol pairs that wanted a shared abstract base, and the two that simply had no dissector at all. Alongside it come the Linux cooked-capture headers and a dissector for each `arphrd_type` they do not read as an EtherType, the IP pseudo-header, verified and computed one's-complement checksums, stricter handling of a malformed IPv6 extension header, and HTTP dispatched to its HTTP/1 and HTTP/2 classes under the RFC names.

- **Added** -- `LinuxSLL1` and `LinuxSLL2`, dissectors for the Linux "cooked" capture headers `tcpdump -i any` writes, under `LINKTYPE_LINUX_SLL` (113) and `LINKTYPE_LINUX_SLL2` (276), on a `LinuxSLL` family base; PCAP and PCAP-NG frames of either link type dispatch to them ([#2438](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2438), [#2443](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2443)). `'LinuxSLL'` selects either version, and each version's own name selects that version alone. `protocol_type` dispatches onward as an EtherType, except under the `arphrd_type` values that carry none -- `FRAD`, `NETLINK`, `IPGRE`/`IP6GRE` and `IEEE80211_RADIOTAP` -- each of which has a dissector of its own, below ([#2460](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2460)), reached through `LinuxSLL`'s own registry keyed by `arphrd_type`, which `register_arphrd()` extends ([#2510](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2510), [#2527](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2527)). The hardware types are `ARPHRD`, generated by `pcapkit.vendor.linux.arphrd.ARPHRD` from a hand-maintained table. It names only the `<linux/if_arp.h>` values that table lists, so any other, `ARPHRD_ETHER` included, reads as `Unassigned` ([#2495](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2495), [#2497](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2497)).

- **Added** -- `FRAD`, a dissector for Frame Relay's multiprotocol encapsulation ([RFC 2427](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc2427), obsoleting [RFC 1490](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc1490)), which `LinuxSLL` dispatches to for `ARPHRD_FRAD` ([#2499](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2499)). The direct NLPID and SNAP-encapsulated forms both dispatch onward, to IPv4/IPv6 and (via the EtherType registry every Ethernet/VLAN/LinuxSLL frame shares) ARP among others; any other NLPID or SNAP OUI is kept as `Raw`, and `register_frad()` registers a dissector for a further NLPID ([#2527](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2527)).

- **Added** -- `Netlink`, a dissector for the fixed 16-octet `nlmsghdr` framing of Linux's kernel/userspace IPC transport (the kernel's `netlink(7)` man page; [RFC 3549 Section 2.3.2](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc3549#section-2.3.2)), which `LinuxSLL` dispatches to for `ARPHRD_NETLINK` ([#2500](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2500), [#2507](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2507)). Netlink is IPC rather than a wire format, so the header's byte order is ambiguous; `nlmsg_byteorder()` guesses it the way Wireshark's netlink dissector does. Only the first message's header is dissected: what follows it, its per-family payload and any further message in the same capture, is kept as `Raw` unless `register_netlink()` registers a dissector for its `nlmsg_type` ([#2527](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2527)).

- **Added** -- `GRE`, a dissector for the Generic Routing Encapsulation base header ([RFC 2784](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc2784)) plus the optional Key and Sequence Number extensions ([RFC 2890](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc2890)), which `LinuxSLL` dispatches to for both `ARPHRD_IPGRE` and `ARPHRD_IP6GRE` -- the same GRE framing either way, the two differing only in the tunnel's outer IP version ([#2501](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2501), [#2508](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2508)). The Protocol Type field dispatches onward as an EtherType, through the family registry (`Link.__proto__`) Ethernet, VLAN and LinuxSLL share. A non-zero Version, [RFC 2637](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc2637)'s Enhanced GRE, is kept as `Raw` rather than misread.

- **Added** -- `Radiotap`, a dissector for the radiotap header (<https://www.radiotap.org/>) capture tools prepend to an 802.11 frame, which `LinuxSLL` dispatches to for `ARPHRD_IEEE80211_RADIOTAP` ([#2502](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2502), [#2509](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2509)). It decodes the fixed 8-octet start (`it_version`, `it_pad`, `it_len`) and records every present-flags word, following bit 31 from one word to the next. The optional fields those words select are kept whole rather than decoded, and the 802.11 MAC frame behind them, which has no dissector in this package yet, as `Raw`.

- **Added** -- `ICMPv4` ([RFC 792](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc792)) and `ICMPv6` ([RFC 4443](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc4443)), on an abstract `ICMP` base, for IP protocols 1 and 58, which were kept as `Raw` ([#2436](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2436), [#2446](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2446)); a protochain names `ICMPv6` where it named `IPv6_ICMP`. Echo, Destination Unreachable, Time Exceeded and Parameter Problem bodies are decoded, and ICMPv6's Packet Too Big; every other type, Neighbor Discovery and MLD included, keeps its body raw. ICMPv4 takes the plain `ICMP` of [RFC 792](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc792) as its `id()` and alias. `register_icmp_type()` registers a message-body parser. The type and code registries are `pcapkit.const.icmp` and `pcapkit.const.icmpv6`, crawled from IANA.

- **Added** -- IGMPv1 ([RFC 1112](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc1112)), IGMPv2 ([RFC 2236](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc2236)) and IGMPv3 ([RFC 3376](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc3376)), as `IGMPv1`, `IGMPv2` and `IGMPv3`, each named after its module ([#2529](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2529), [#2542](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2542)), on an abstract `IGMP` base bound at IP protocol 2, which picks the version from the message type alone except for a Membership Query: one of 8 octets is IGMPv1 if its Max Resp Time is zero and IGMPv2 otherwise, one of 12 or more IGMPv3 ([#2439](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2439), [#2448](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2448), [#2444](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2444), [#2465](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2465)). An IGMPv3 Query carries its source list and a Membership Report its group records, and an IGMPv3 Query's Max Resp Code and QQIC are decoded from their floating-point form. Any other message, a 9- to 11-octet Query included, is kept as `Raw`. IGMPv1 takes the plain `IGMP` of [RFC 1112](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc1112) as its `id()` and alias, and IGMPv2's and IGMPv3's `id()` are their own names alone, `('IGMPv2',)` and `('IGMPv3',)`; the base's is `('IGMP', 'IGMPv2', 'IGMPv3')`, each member's canonical name ([#2512](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2512), [#2506](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2506), [#2518](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2518)). The registries are `pcapkit.const.igmp`: the types crawled from IANA, the group record types from a hand-maintained table.

- **Added** -- `TLS` (SSLv3 and TLS 1.0 to 1.3) at TCP 443 and `DTLS` (DTLS 1.0, 1.2 and 1.3) at UDP 5684, on an abstract `SSL` base ([#2437](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2437), [#2441](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2441)). Each reads every version of its record layer and reports it through `alias`, as `ARP` does by operation, telling 1.3 apart by its `supported_versions` extension; `id()` stays `('TLS',)` or `('DTLS',)`. A cleartext handshake record is decoded -- ClientHello and ServerHello down to their cipher suites, compression methods and extension types, any other message at type and length -- as are an alert and ChangeCipherSpec, while encrypted records stay opaque octets. Each whole TLS record in a TCP segment is a layer of its own, the previous record's payload, so each alias names its own version -- `TCP:TLS 1.3:TLS 1.2` -- and one that fails to decode is `Raw` with the rest of the segment ([#2517](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2517), [#2537](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2537)). `SSLv3` exists for explicit construction only. A record split across TCP segments is not reassembled and stays `Raw`. DTLS also reads and builds DTLS 1.3's unified-header records ([RFC 9147 Section 4.1](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc9147#section-4.1)), except one carrying a connection ID, which stays Raw as an [RFC 9146](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc9146) `tls12_cid` record does ([#2467](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2467), [#2494](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2494)). The content and handshake types are `pcapkit.const.tls`, crawled from IANA ([#2495](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2495), [#2503](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2503)).

- **Added** -- `SSLv2`, a dissector for SSL version 2 (draft-hickman-netscape-ssl-00), whose record layer shares nothing with TLS's ([#2457](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2457), [#2466](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2466)). It is bound to no port: on TCP 443 a segment's first record that TLS rejects is tried as SSLv2, and kept only as a complete CLIENT-HELLO or SERVER-HELLO whose length fields agree, so anything else stays `Raw`; a kept one drops the rejected TLS trial's warnings ([#2525](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2525), [#2537](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2537)). Its constants are `pcapkit.const.sslv2`, from a hand-maintained table.

- **Added** -- `pcapkit.foundation.reassembly.dtls.DTLS`, exported as `DTLS_Reassembly`, which reassembles a DTLS handshake message fragmented across records ([RFC 6347 Section 4.2.3](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc6347#section-4.2.3)), keyed by the UDP 4-tuple and the epoch, accepting overlapping fragments and holding a buffer until it completes ([#2440](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2440), [#2451](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2451)). It is opt-in, as `dtls=True` with `reassembly=True` to `Extractor` or `extract()`, and runs on the default engine alone, fed by `dtls_reassembly()` and its `pcapkit.toolkit.pcapng` counterpart from the first record of each datagram only; any other engine turns it off with a warning. `dtls_handshake()` decodes a reassembled message ([#2496](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2496), [#2528](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2528)).

- **Added** -- `IP_Pseudo`, the IPv4 ([RFC 793](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc793)) or IPv6 ([RFC 8200 Section 8.1](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8200#section-8.1)) pseudo-header an upper-layer checksum covers, built from an IPv4 or IPv6 layer, explicit values, or both ([#2471](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2471), [#2479](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2479)). From a layer it takes the final destination of a source route or of a Routing header with segments left, a Home Address option's address as the IPv6 source, and the upper-layer length, from UDP's Length or a jumbogram's Jumbo Payload Length where those apply; a fragment of a larger packet, or a header it cannot walk at any depth of the chain, raises `ProtocolError` ([#2513](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2513), [#2543](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2543)). It is not a layer on the wire.

- **Added** -- verification and computation of the one's-complement Internet checksum ([RFC 1071](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc1071)) of the IPv4 header, TCP, UDP, ICMP, ICMPv6 and OSPFv2, in `pcapkit.protocols.misc.checksum.checksum` ([#2470](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2470), [#2472](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2472), [#2473](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2473), [#2474](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2474), [#2475](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2475), [#2543](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2543)). Parsing verifies only under `PCAPKIT_CHECKSUM_VERIFY`, off by default, recording a `ChecksumStatus` as `checksum_status` and warning on a mismatch with `ChecksumWarning`, a `ProtocolWarning` a filter can silence alone. Construction computes a checksum the caller leaves out, unless `PCAPKIT_CHECKSUM_COMPUTE` is off. An IPv4 or IPv6 layer verifies and computes its upper layer's checksum over the `IP_Pseudo` it builds, and `make()` of TCP, UDP and ICMPv6 also takes a `pseudo`. What checksum offload leaves on the sending host is `NOT_COMPUTED` rather than a mismatch; a fragment's TCP, UDP, ICMP or ICMPv6 checksum, which covers octets the fragment lacks, is `NO_PSEUDO_HEADER`.

- **Added** -- `register_ipx()`, `register_loopback()`, `register_sctp_chunk()`, `register_sctp_parameter()` and `register_sctp_cause()`, wrapping IPX's packet-type map, Loopback's address-family map and SCTP's chunk, chunk-parameter and error-cause parser maps with the validation the other wrappers carry, and the `IPX.register` and `Loopback.register` classmethods the IPX and Loopback wrappers call ([#2511](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2511), [#2521](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2521)).

- **Changed** -- **a breaking change to** the IPv6 extension-header walk: a known extension header whose dedicated parser raises is now the upper layer, parsed once as `Raw`, and the walk stops there, as [RFC 8200 Section 4](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8200#section-4) has a node discard such a packet ([#2477](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2477), [#2480](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2480)). It used to fall back to the generic `IPv6_Ext` and walk on; `IPv6_Ext` now parses only Shim6, which has no dedicated parser, and `IPv6.__generic_ext_codes__`, the headers that fell back, is removed. The library no longer passes `IPv6_Ext.read()` an `error=`, so `IPv6_Ext.error` is `None` unless a caller supplies one; the `Raw` layer's `info.error` holds the failure instead.

- **Changed** -- **a breaking change to** construction: a checksum left out of `make()` is computed rather than zero, so the octets built change ([#2471](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2471), [#2543](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2543)). `IPv4` computes its header checksum, an IPv4 or IPv6 layer that of a TCP or UDP payload built without one, and `OSPF` that of an OSPFv2 packet. A checksum given is written as given, and turning `PCAPKIT_CHECKSUM_COMPUTE` off restores zero.

- **Changed** -- **a breaking change to** `DRARP`: it moves from `pcapkit.protocols.application.rarp` to `pcapkit.protocols.application.drarp`, with no re-export at the old path, as a module holds one protocol class ([#2529](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2529), [#2540](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2540)). `pcapkit.DRARP`, `pcapkit.protocols.DRARP` and a parsed RARP's `DRARP` alias are unchanged.

- **Changed** -- **a breaking change to** HTTP: parsing octets through `HTTP`, building it from fields and its `from_data` give the `HTTPv1` or `HTTPv2` member, never an instance of the base, and so does a TCP or UDP payload on an HTTP port, whose data sits under `httpv1` or `httpv2` where it sat under `http` ([#2524](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2524), [#2544](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2544)). The members are renamed after their modules, `httpv1.HTTP` and `httpv2.HTTP` becoming `HTTPv1` and `HTTPv2` along with their schema and data classes, with no re-export at the old paths ([#2529](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2529)); `pcapkit.HTTPv1`, `pcapkit.HTTPv2` and the package exports of those names are unchanged. `id()` takes the RFC names, `('HTTP/1',)` and `('HTTP/2',)`, and the base's lists both, where they were `('HTTP', 'HTTPv1')`, `('HTTP', 'HTTPv2')` and `('HTTP', 'HTTPv1', 'HTTPv2')`. So a layer no longer compares equal to `'HTTP'`, `'HTTPv1'` or `'HTTPv2'`; `'HTTP'` still selects either version, and each version's own name, `'HTTP/1'` or `'HTTPv1'` say, selects it alone, where any HTTP layer matched `'HTTPv1'` and `'HTTPv2'` both. On the transport path a payload neither version recognises, a bare connection preface included, is `Raw` with no `error`, where it carried `'HTTP: unknown version'` or `'HTTP/2: connection preface with no frame'`; a direct `HTTP(...)` on it still raises `ProtocolError`. One that passes for an HTTP/2 frame header but `HTTPv2` refuses carries that parser's own error on either path, where it was `'HTTP: unknown version'`. `HTTPv2` itself now parses a payload opening with the connection preface, kept as `preface`, and its `make()` takes `preface`.

- **Changed** -- **a breaking change to** IPv6 reassembly: a fragment overlapping another of its datagram, other than an exact duplicate, abandons the datagram, reported as `Completion.OVERLAP`, as [RFC 8200 Section 4.5](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8200#section-4.5) requires; 1.5.x overwrote the octets it overlapped, as IPv4 still does under [RFC 791](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc791) ([#2547](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2547), [#2569](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2569)). `Buffer` gains a required `extent` field.

- **Changed** -- **a breaking change to** TCP reassembly's gap cap: `pcapkit.foundation.reassembly.tcp.TCP.__window__` is removed with no alias, and the cap is `TCP_GAP_LIMIT`, set by `PCAPKIT_TCP_GAP_LIMIT`, 16 MiB by default as before and `-1` for no limit ([#2546](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2546), [#2579](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2579)). It is a safety limit, read each time `TCP.reassembly` runs, and `-1` or a value above the default warns with `ConfigWarning`.

- **Changed** -- **a breaking change to** `Extractor` and `extract()`: their `dtls` parameter, above, follows `tcp`, so a positional argument from `buffer_size` on moves one place along, and `ReassemblyManager` takes a required `dtls` ([#2496](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2496), [#2528](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2528)).

- **Changed** -- protocol class bodies are reordered to the member-order convention, with no change in behaviour, and a project test guards the order ([#2490](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2490), [#2522](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2522), [#2523](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2523), [#2538](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2538), [#2539](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2539), [#2541](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2541)).

- **Changed** -- the house conventions record that a protocol class takes its module's name, one to a module ([#2529](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2529), [#2542](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2542)); that a versioned family's base dispatches on every path, so parsing, building and `from_data` give a member rather than the base ([#2524](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2524), [#2544](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2544)); that a docstring or prose block carries one `versionadded` per version, without a body that only names the thing added ([#2530](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2530), [#2542](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2542)); and that each class-level registry map has a wrapper in `pcapkit.foundation.registry` ([#2511](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2511), [#2521](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2521)). `const` is a label added alongside a commit type rather than a type, in the pull request template and `CONTRIBUTING.md` too, and a span the transport registries assign to a service keeps its bare name, the mint criterion's exception to code-suffixing ([#2549](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2549), [#2550](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2550), [#2570](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2570)). The registry conventions record the exceptions to their case-folding rules -- the crawlers that re-case the names they generate, the `Method` constructor's fold, and `Command.get` and `Method.get` answering a name miss with an unregistered member -- and the folds that live outside any registry ([#2551](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2551), [#2552](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2552), [#2553](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2553), [#2556](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2556), [#2565](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2565), [#2582](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2582), [#2583](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2583)).

- **Changed** -- a project test fails on a shipped module with no documentation entry, the placeholder sources under `NotImplemented/` excepted, and the modules this release adds that had none -- `pcapkit.const.linux`, the `pcapkit.vendor.tls` and `pcapkit.vendor.sslv2` crawlers and the checksum packages -- now have one ([#2559](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2559), [#2584](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2584)).

- **Changed** -- the CPython unit-test legs run `pytest -n logical`, a worker per logical CPU, where `-n auto` counted physical cores and ran 2 workers on the 4-CPU runner; the weekly CI timing report reads the latest Python's leg too ([#2571](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2571), [#2572](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2572)).

- **Changed** -- a pull request no longer adds its own changelog bullet: an unreleased version's entries are written centrally by the maintainer, and `CONTRIBUTING.md` and the pull request template say so ([#2449](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2449), [#2483](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2483)).

- **Fixed** -- `AppType.get` takes the `default` `EnumLookup.get` advertises, a registered member's value that it never mints, and a miss raises `EnumValueError`, still a `ValueError`, where it raised a builtin one ([#2545](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2545), [#2568](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2568)).

- **Fixed** -- under `beholder`, a next layer captured at full length whose parse raised, such as a malformed TCP header, reported its warnings before being replaced with `Raw`. Its trial now holds them however much was captured and drops them with the layer, so a kept layer's warnings follow those of the layers it carries ([#2516](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2516), [#2519](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2519)).

- **Fixed** -- the PyPCAPFile engine's header checks reported the warnings of a trial parse they rejected. The extension-header trial now drops them as under `beholder`, and the IPv4 and TCP header check holds its trial's and discards them when it raises ([#2526](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2526), [#2577](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2577)).

- **Fixed** -- selecting `'IPv6_Ext'` matched none of the dedicated extension headers: `IPv6_Ext.id()` now lists its direct concrete subclasses -- HOPOPT, IPv6_Route, IPv6_Frag, ESP, AH, IPv6_Opts, MH and HIP -- each of which keeps its own name alone, as AH and ESP do under IPsec ([#2462](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2462), [#2464](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2464)).

- **Fixed** -- a membership test such as `'ESP' in layer` on an extension header parsed by the IPv6 header's walk -- AH, HOPOPT, IPv6_Route, IPv6_Frag, IPv6_Opts, MH, HIP or the generic IPv6_Ext -- raised `AttributeError`. The layer now holds `NoPayload`, the upper layer belonging to the enclosing IPv6 header ([#2484](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2484), [#2487](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/issues/2487), [#2486](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2486), [#2488](https://github.com/JarryShaw/PyPCAPKit/pull/2488)).
