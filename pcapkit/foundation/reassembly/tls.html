TLS Record Reassembly¶

pcapkit.foundation.reassembly.tls.reassembly contains TLS only, which reconstructs the TLS records of a TCP stream, a record split across segments included (RFC 9846 Section 5).

Added in version 1.9.0.

class pcapkit.foundation.reassembly.tls.reassembly.TLS[_PT: Info, _DT: Info, _IT: tuple, _BT: Info](*, strict=True, store=True, timeout=None)[source]¶

Bases: ReassemblyBase[Packet, Datagram[_AT], tuple[_AT, int, _AT, int], Buffer], Generic[_AT]

Reassembly for TLS records.

Parameters:

  • strict (bool) – if True, report a record that is not completely reassembled as the tuple of its fragment’s received runs; otherwise as one contiguous fragment, its holes zero-filled

  • store (bool) – if store reassembled datagram in memory, i.e., self._dtgram (if not, datagram will be discarded after callback)

  • timeout (float | None) – reassembly timeout in seconds, on the capture’s own clock; None selects __timeout__, which for TLS disables expiry

Note

Over TCP a record is delimited by its header alone (RFC 9846 Section 5.1), so each direction of a connection is followed as one octet stream, from a record boundary, and cut into records by TLS.record_header. Every record is reported, the ones a segment holds whole included, since a boundary is known only from the record before it.

The stream is the segments’ octets in sequence order (RFC 9293 Section 3.4). A segment ahead of the octets held waits for those before it, and of octets received twice the first are kept (RFC 9293 Section 3.10). A record is reported once whole. One the stream ends inside – at its FIN, an RST or the end of the capture – is reported incomplete, and so is one a lost segment left a hole in, as the records after it are.

The Extractor feeds it TCP reassembly’s reasm.tcp.packet of every segment on a port TCP.__proto__ binds to TLS, through pcapkit.toolkit.pcap.tls_reassembly() and its siblings, and tls_record() decodes what it reassembles. A complete record’s packet holds the same decode, run on first read; both call the protocol’s analyze_record(), the packet that of __protocol_type__.

Warning

Where the stream starts. With its SYN captured, the stream is followed from the octet after it, a record boundary. Without it, and again after octets that open no record, the boundary is a guess: the first segment arriving whose payload opens a record header, by TLS.record_header, is taken to open a record, and the octets before it are not reported. Octets past a hole that covers a record header cannot be cut into records, and are not reported either.

What the guess can get wrong. The header’s length may not exceed 2 ** 14 + 2048 (RFC 5246 Section 6.2.3), so a segment whose first octets declare a longer record, as 17 03 03 ff ff would, opens no stream. Ciphertext or a record’s tail whose first five octets read as a header within that bound still does: the records cut from it are false, and those of the real stream behind it, up to the next octets that open no record, are lost.

Where it ends. A FIN or an RST closes the stream, and a later segment on the same endpoints is ignored unless it is a SYN.

Example

>>> from pcapkit.foundation.reassembly.tls.reassembly import TLS
# Initialise instance:
>>> tls_reassembly = TLS()
# Call reassembly:
>>> tls_reassembly(packet_dict)
# Fetch result:
>>> result = tls_reassembly.datagram

Algorithm¶

See also

A record over TCP is delimited by its header alone (RFC 9846 Section 5.1), and the stream is the segments’ octets in sequence order (RFC 9293 Section 3.4).

Each direction of a connection is a buffer, which holds the octets past the next record’s boundary as runs in sequence order. A segment’s new octets join the runs, and the records whole from the boundary on are cut off and reported:

        flowchart TD
    SEG["a segment, as TCP reassembly records it"]
    SYN{"SYN?"}
    OPEN["a fresh buffer, its boundary<br/>the octet after the SYN"]
    HAS{"a buffer?"}
    SYNC{"its payload opens<br/>a record header?"}
    DROP["ignored"]
    HOLD["hold the octets no earlier<br/>segment brought"]
    HEAD{"a header held<br/>at the boundary?"}
    BAD{"it opens a record?"}
    GONE["the buffer is dropped"]
    WHOLE{"the record whole?"}
    CUT["report it COMPLETE, and<br/>move the boundary past it"]
    END{"an RST, or a FIN with<br/>every octet before it?"}
    CLOSE["submit the buffer, and<br/>close the stream"]
    WAIT["wait for the next segment"]

    SEG --> SYN
    SYN -->|yes| OPEN --> HOLD
    SYN -->|no| HAS
    HAS -->|open| HOLD
    HAS -->|closed| DROP
    HAS -->|none| SYNC
    SYNC -->|no| DROP
    SYNC -->|"yes: a buffer from it"| HOLD
    HOLD --> HEAD
    HEAD -->|no| END
    HEAD -->|yes| BAD
    BAD -->|no| GONE
    BAD -->|yes| WHOLE
    WHOLE -->|yes| CUT --> HEAD
    WHOLE -->|no| END
    END -->|yes| CLOSE
    END -->|no| WAIT

Without the SYN, the boundary a buffer opens at is a guess, which the length bound of TLS.record_header narrows but cannot make safe; the class’s warning above says what it rules out.

submit() reports what a buffer still holds, record by record from the boundary: one whose fragment is whole COMPLETE, and one with a hole PARTIAL, or TIMEOUT once expired. It stops at the first hole a header lies in.

Terminology¶

The reassembly is fed reasm.tcp.packet, TCP reassembly’s record of a segment.

reasm.tls.datagram¶

Data structure for reassembled TLS record (element of TLS.datagram) is Datagram – completed, the original record identifier, the packet numbers that carried it, the record header’s content_type, version and length, fragment, the reassembled fragment (a contiguous bytes, or – under strict=True and an incomplete record – a tuple of the octet runs actually received), and packet, what tls_record() decodes the record to. packet is decoded on first read, as a DTLS message’s is, and is None for an incomplete record.

reasm.tls.buffer¶

Data structure for internal buffering (TLS._buffer) is Buffer – one direction of a TCP connection, keyed by its BufferID: the next record’s boundary, the octets held past it as Run objects, the octets each packet added, and where the stream ends.
